Reconstructing a flow field from a handful of patches

This is the Vision Transformer Masked Autoencoder from my Master's thesis, running live in the browser. The model sees only the patches you make visible and reconstructs the entire velocity field from them - click or drag on the middle panel to choose what it gets to see.

What the model is doing

The snapshot is a \( 96 \times 192 \) field of velocity components \( (u, v) \) from the wake behind two adjacent cylinders, cut into a \( 4 \times 8 \) grid of \( 24 \times 24 \) patches. The flow state in the visible region and the position of these patches are embedded and passed through the encoder transformer, allowing the model to process the available information. The decoder transformer then works to fill in the gaps, figuring out what is in the missing regions.

With the default 25% pattern the model is working from two columns of patches, and it still recovers the vortex street downstream of them - the wake is highly structured, and the attention maps learn where that structure comes from.

To read more about the theory, read my thesis here.

ViT vs t-ViT

The ViT sees a single snapshot. The t-ViT instead sees the same patches across \( N \) snapshots spaced \( \Delta t \) steps apart (here \( N=5, \Delta t = 10 \)), ending at the one shown, and attends over them causally - each timestep can only attend to itself and to earlier ones, but not to the future. The extra temporal context lets the model disambiguate flow states that look almost identical in a single frame - time is an important component of the Navier Stokes equations that cannot be ignored.

Both models run entirely in your browser via ONNX Runtime Web - nothing is sent to a server. They are quantised to int8 to keep the download reasonable, so the reconstruction is very slightly noisier than the full-precision model reported in the thesis.
